Formula: CAGR = ((Final ÷ Initial)1/years − 1) × 100 — the steady annual rate that would take the start value to the end value if growth were perfectly smooth.

Questions investors ask about CAGR

What does CAGR hide?

CAGR compresses a bumpy journey into one smooth annual rate, so it hides volatility, drawdowns and the order of gains and losses. A fund that shows 12% CAGR may still have fallen 30% mid-way. Two investments with identical CAGR can feel very different to hold.

How is CAGR different from absolute return?

Absolute return is simply the total change over the whole period — double your money and it reads +100%, no matter how long that took. CAGR divides that growth across the years (2.4x over 8 years is about 11.6% p.a.), which is what makes investments with different horizons comparable.

What counts as a good CAGR?

It depends on the asset and the risk taken to earn it. Mid-to-high single digits is reasonable for debt, while 12–14% p.a. has been a fair long-run expectation from Indian equity. Treat any CAGR far above that as a flag to check what risk produced it, not as a floor for future returns.
