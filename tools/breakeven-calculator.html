The formula: Long call BE = Strike + Premium + (Charges per lot ÷ Lot size) · Long put BE = Strike − Premium − (Charges per lot ÷ Lot size) · Move % needed = |BE − Spot| ÷ Spot × 100. Charges are entered per lot (brokerage + STT + exchange fees together) and divided by lot size to get the per-unit impact; leave at 0 to ignore them.

Questions traders ask about break-even

What is the break-even point of a long call?

For a long call, the break-even at expiry is the strike price plus the premium you paid (plus any charges per unit). The underlying must close above that price for the trade to end with a profit.

Do brokerage and charges really change my break-even?

Yes. Charges are billed per lot, so their per-unit impact is charges divided by lot size. That amount is added to a call's break-even and subtracted from a put's — small on Nifty, meaningful on low-premium contracts.

Is break-even a profit guarantee?

No. Break-even assumes the price is reached at expiry. Before expiry, time decay (theta) erodes premium every day, so the underlying usually must move further, and sooner, than the break-even number suggests.
