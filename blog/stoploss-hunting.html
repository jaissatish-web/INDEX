Why Your Stoploss Keeps Getting Hit — and How to Read the Hunt Before It Happens

You place the stoploss below support. Price comes exactly there, takes it out, and then reverses in your direction. That is not bad luck. It is a system — and once you see the system, you stop being its fuel.

SJ Dalal Desk · Nov 2026 · 9 min read Checking market…

Every trader knows this scene. You buy near a support and keep the stoploss a little below it — with a "small buffer". The market falls straight into that buffer, hits your stop, and then turns and climbs without you. You blame luck. You blame the market. But this happens to lakhs of traders every single day in the same places — and the same places get hit every single day. That is not chance. That is design.

Here is the one line that changes how you see charts forever: the market cannot move without stoplosses. A big player who wants to take a large position needs someone on the other side to fill it. Yours and everyone else's stops — sitting predictably at the same levels — are exactly that other side. Where fresh stops are available, the market goes there. Where the stops are already finished, nothing much happens.

First, the two habits that make stops predictable

Two intentions run this market, and both are visible in your own head:

  • The buyer wants the bottom. After one index has already run up, the buyer thinks: "this one has gone too far — the other index is sitting near its support, more room left there." So the buy happens in the other index.
  • The seller wants the top. He missed the last top, so he waits for the next one to short.

Because thousands of people share these two habits, every level collects a cluster of stops around it — buy stops just below supports, sell stops just above resistances. Those clusters are the addresses the market visits.

The levels where the crowd parks its stops

Stoploss hunting is not random. It happens at the levels every chart shows:

  • The opening candle of the day. Its low becomes the day's first support, its high the first resistance — the very first cluster of the session.
  • Yesterday's high and low. The whole market watches these.
  • Every visible support and resistance on the popular timeframes.

None of this is secret. That is exactly the point — a level that everyone can see is a level where everyone's stops sit together. One pool, one address.

The "breakout" that was only a hunt

Now the most valuable candle picture in trading. Price rises to a resistance. Two candles poke above it — excitement everywhere, "breakout!" — but both close back below. Then price falls.

What really happened? Nothing was breaking out. The market pushed above the level only to collect the sell stops parked there — the stops of everyone who shorted earlier at the top. Once the fuel was taken, the push had no reason to continue, and price reversed. A wick above resistance is not a breakout. It is a collection drive. Only a close — held after the candle completes — tells you the move is real.

Two indices, one game

Here is the part almost nobody teaches. Watch Nifty and Bank Nifty together and you will see money rotate: after one index runs hard, buyers move to the other index because it "looks cheaper" and sits near its support. Big players know this habit too. When they want to sell, they do it where fresh stops are still available — often in the other index, right where the crowd just built positions. The market reverses there, exactly because that is where the fuel is.

And the old trap repeats: buying the index that already looks weak "because it has more room" — after it has already fallen — is buying into a place where the stops below have often already been eaten. The crowd makes this mistake again and again. Do not be the crowd.

The greed move and the retest

After the stops are collected, the market often gives a small taste of the "real" direction — just enough to pull in the traders waiting for a retest. Those entries become the next cluster. One more collection. Then the actual move begins — the move that was never possible until the fuel was ready.

We learned this the hard way, with our own money: a day of repeated failed breakouts, the sentiment screaming "upside!", positions rebuilding at the same level again and again — and each rebuild was another pool for the hunt. That single day taught more than a month of winners.

What we actually do now

  • Before entering, ask one question: who is trapped here? If you cannot answer where the stops are, you are not ready to trade.
  • Breakout = close, not peek. A candle must close beyond the level and hold. Everything else is collection.
  • Do not park the stop inside the obvious cluster. Place it by structure — and size the position so that a wick alone cannot remove you.
  • Do not buy the "cheap" index after the strong one ran. Weak-looking does not mean ready. It often means the fuel below is already burnt.
  • When the map is unclear, the trade is zero. A skipped trap costs nothing. An entered trap costs a month.
"The market doesn't move to where the analysis is. It moves to where the stops are."

Check yourself

Q1. Price wicks above resistance and closes back below it. What actually happened?

The sell stops parked above the resistance were collected. It was a stop hunt, not a breakout — expect reversal unless a candle closes and holds above the level.

Q2. One index has already run up hard. Buyers are entering the other index "near its support". Who benefits from that?

The player who wants to sell — the fresh positions in the other index come with fresh stops just below, and that is exactly the fuel a large seller needs.

Q3. Price has already fallen far below a support. Can the market "hunt stops" there again?

Mostly no — the stops below that support were already consumed on the way down. No fuel, no visit. The market goes where fresh stops are available.

📌 Today's cheat sheet

  • LAWThe market cannot move without stoplosses. Fresh stops = fuel. No fresh stops = no move.
  • MAPStops cluster just below supports, just above resistances, at the opening candle's high–low, and at yesterday's high–low.
  • FAKEA peek above resistance that closes back below is collection, not breakout. Read the close.
  • ROTATEAfter one index runs, the crowd buys the other "cheap" one — right where big players need fuel. Don't be the crowd.
  • SLNever park inside the obvious cluster. Structure-based stop, position sized for the wick.
  • ASKBefore any entry: who is trapped here, and where are their stops?
